Phishing Awareness Program

Can you spot the fake email?

Phishing is the most common way attackers get into accounts. This 15-minute program takes you through a quick quiz, a short lesson, and a second quiz so you can see what you learned.

Step 3: Learn the red flags

Phishing emails try to rush you into acting. Slow down and check these five things.

  • 1. Sender address

    The display name can say anything. Check the real address. Look-alikes such as helpdesk@uni-support.co instead of your real domain are a warning.

  • 2. Urgency or fear

    "Act in 24 hours or your account is closed." Pressure is the attacker's main tool.

  • 3. Mismatched links

    Hover (or long-press on a phone) to preview the real destination. If it doesn't match the text, don't click.

  • 4. Unexpected attachments

    Invoices, "scanned documents" and ZIP files you didn't ask for are a classic way to deliver malware.

  • 5. Requests for secrets

    Real organisations never ask for your password, OTP or PIN by email, text or call.

Quick check: real or fake?

I think I clicked, what now?

  1. Don't panic, and don't delete the email yet. It is useful evidence.
  2. Disconnect from Wi-Fi if you opened an attachment.
  3. Change the password of the affected account from a safe device, and anywhere you reused it.
  4. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) and check for unknown sign-ins or forwarding rules.
  5. Report it to your IT or security team, and forward the email to them.
  6. If money or card details were involved, call your bank at once. In India, report cyber fraud at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
